Pre-Query Intent

Independent notes on AI judgment, gateways, and orchestration above models.

By Pre-query Layer

In these notes, pre-query intent refers to the context and emerging intent that precede a fully formed, explicit user question. It includes how a system may interpret signals and help shape question formation before an answer is requested.

An AI gateway may select and route requests among models, agents, tools, and services. Multi-model orchestration coordinates those resources under constraints such as cost, latency, permissions, policy, and verification.

These notes also examine interoperability and governance. Neutral coordination is a conditional possibility, not an inevitable outcome: open protocols, standards bodies, cloud platforms, or consortia may address the same problems.

AI has been competing on answers.

But the most consequential layer may be forming somewhere else.

As models, agents, search systems, tools, devices, and services multiply, intelligence becomes abundant. And when intelligence becomes abundant, a different question begins to matter:

Who decides which intelligence gets to act?

Before an answer, there is selection.
Before selection, there is judgment.
Before judgment, intent must somehow be understood.

The system that occupies this position may not need to be the best model. It may instead determine which model, agent, search system, tool, or service gets the opportunity to answer or act.

That changes the competitive question.

The model owns capability. The gateway owns choice.

These notes examine the emerging territory of pre-query intent, context, judgment, multi-model orchestration, agent selection, AI gateways, control planes, interoperability, and ecosystem neutrality—and the possibility that the next strategic layer of AI may emerge not inside the models, but above and before them.

Before the Question: The Next Competitive Layer in AI

What if the next major AI competition begins before the user has fully formed a question?

The AI industry has spent years competing over who can produce the best answer. But an increasingly context-aware system may act before an explicit prompt exists—interpreting signals, inferring intent, recognizing what matters, and helping determine what should be asked in the first place.

If that happens, question formation itself becomes a competitive layer.

The strategic question is no longer only:

Who has the best answer?

It also becomes:

Who gets closest to the moment before the question exists?

This essay explores pre-query intent and the possibility that AI competition may move upstream—from answering human questions toward understanding and shaping the conditions from which those questions emerge.

The competition for answers may be only part of the competition.

Above the Models: The Coming Battle for the AI Flag

What happens when intelligence itself is no longer scarce?

As models and agents proliferate, the most strategically important position may not belong to the intelligence that produces the best answer.

It may belong to the layer that decides who gets to answer.

Such a layer would interpret context and intent, exercise judgment, orchestrate multiple intelligences, and determine which model, agent, search system, tool, or service should act in a given situation.

At that point, orchestration is no longer merely a technical problem.

It becomes a question of selection, control, interoperability, neutrality, and ecosystem power.

And if many competing intelligences must pass through a gateway before reaching the user, another question follows:

Who owns the gateway?

This essay examines the emerging competition above models and agents—and why the battle for AI leadership may eventually extend beyond intelligence itself to the layer that chooses among intelligences.

Model leadership does not guarantee gateway leadership.
